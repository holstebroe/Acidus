# TB-303 Emulation — Parameter Confidence Table

This document cross-references the actual constants used in `src/core/` (`Oscillator`, `Filter`, `Envelope`, `SynthEngine`) against `TB303_RESEARCH_COMPENDIUM.md`. It answers two questions: **which numbers in the code are close to documented/measured hardware values (safe to trust), and which are tuned-by-feel placeholders (worth experimenting with until the "Faithful" engine matches a real 303)?**

Confidence levels:

- 🟢 **Confirmed** — matches a factory-service-notes value or a primary circuit analysis directly.
- 🟡 **Estimate** — a plausible value, order-of-magnitude or mechanism confirmed, exact number not independently sourced.
- 🔴 **Unsourced / free parameter** — no source found; treat as a pure calibration knob.

Both the "Accurate" and "Faithful" engines share the oscillator (`Oscillator.cpp`) and the CV-summing stage (`SynthEngine.cpp`); they differ only in the filter core (`Filter::processAccurateSample` vs `Filter::processFaithfulSample`). Where a row applies to only one filter mode, it's noted in the Notes column.

> **2026-09 regression & correction.** A first pass at two "biggest opportunities" below (the filter's resonance coupling-pole corner, and the oscillator's coupling HPF) turned out to be badly wrong in practice, based on over-reading two thin secondary-source summaries without being able to listen to the result. Both were reverted after direct A/B comparison against **RobinSchmidt/Open303** (`github.com/RobinSchmidt/Open303`, `Source/DSPCode/rosic_Open303.cpp`/`rosic_TeeBeeFilter.h`), a well-regarded, independently ear/measurement-tuned open-source TB-303 emulation that ships its own version of both stages with concrete numbers. Where this document cites an Open303 value below, treat it as meaningfully more trustworthy than an unverified "best guess" (though still not a factory-documented spec) — it's a second, working implementation's real, shipped constant, not just another guess.

## Summary: what's most trustworthy vs. most worth experimenting with

**High confidence — leave alone unless a reference recording disagrees:**
- Slide time constant (60 ms)
- A=110 Hz / 4:1 octave VCO calibration
- Accent Sweep RC (47 ms) and Accent→VCA RC (1.55 ms) time constants
- Square duty-cycle endpoints (45%→70%)
- Accent-VCA-as-control-current-summation (not fixed dB) — already implemented correctly
- Dual-gang Resonance → Accent Sweep coupling (mechanism, not exact blend weight)

**Biggest opportunities to improve accuracy (in priority order):**
1. ~~**Feedback HPF frequency**~~ — **Attempted 2026-09, caused a regression, reverted 2026-09.** See the Faithful-mode filter table above for the full history. `Filter::processFaithfulSample` is back to a single one-pole HPF anchored at 150 Hz (+ up to 100 Hz with Resonance), cross-validated against RobinSchmidt/Open303's shipped 150 Hz value. `Filter::processAccurateSample` was never touched (frozen, already-liked-sounding mode).
2. ~~**Square-only 150 Hz HPF in the oscillator**~~ — **Attempted 2026-09, caused a regression (worse than #1 — it hit both engine modes), reverted 2026-09.** See the Oscillator table above. `Oscillator::processNextSample` is back to a single, fixed (non-pitch-tracking) coupling HPF at ~44.5 Hz, cross-validated against Open303's shipped 44.486 Hz "pre-filter highpass" value.
3. **Add Open303-style low-frequency notch/allpass stages** *outside* the resonance feedback loop (Faithful mode): Open303 uses a ~7.5 Hz notch (bandwidth ~4.7) and a ~14 Hz allpass in series with the signal, separate from its feedback-loop highpass. This is a plausible, concretely-templated way to actually deliver on the "6 further poles" research claim without repeating the 2026-09 mistake of putting a low-frequency pole inside the resonance loop's gain path. Not attempted this round — flagged as the most promising next step, to be added and tested carefully (ideally with real listening, not just stability tests) rather than in the same pass as this regression fix.
4. **Resonance→cutoff 15% CV bleed** in `SynthEngine.cpp`: not corroborated by any source at all. Exposed as a CLAP parameter (`Res->Cutoff Bleed`, range 0–30%, default 15%) — worth an A/B listening test with it set to 0% to check whether it's doing anything useful versus the (also present) Accent-Sweep/Resonance-gang interaction.
5. **Saw 14 kHz LPF + quadratic bend, and the general shape of the oscillator waveshaper**: unsourced but low-risk to leave as-is (cheap, plausible-sounding, not contradicted — just not confirmed). Not exposed as a CLAP parameter this round.

**A cautionary note on this whole document's methodology:** items 1 and 2 above are a concrete example of the risk described at the top of this file — a plausible-sounding correction, derived from secondary-source text without the ability to listen to the result, made the emulation sound *less* like a TB-303, not more. Where a well-regarded reference implementation's actual shipped value is available (as it was here, via Open303), prefer it over a textual secondary-source claim, and treat any further from-scratch "correction" attempt with real caution until it's been validated by ear against a reference recording.

## Important parameter values that are **not emulated at all**

These are documented hardware behaviors (confirmed or reasonably well-supported by the research) with **no corresponding code** in this project. (Sequencer clock/gate/slide timing is intentionally excluded from this list: this project only emulates the sound engine, not the step sequencer — a host/DAW is responsible for note/gate timing.)

1. **6-bit pitch DAC quantization.** The real hardware pitch CV is generated by a 6-bit DAC (≈83 mV/semitone-class resolution before the ±3 mV trim tolerance). The oscillator here uses full double-precision floating point, i.e. effectively infinite pitch resolution — a strictly "smoother" instrument than the original in this one respect.
2. **Component-level variation and calibration drift.** `TB303_EMULATION_REFERENCE.md` §49 explicitly recommends small, slow per-instance variation in transistor Vbe/beta, capacitor value/leakage, resistor tolerance, and trim-pot settings for realism. None of this is implemented — every parameter is deterministic and identical run to run.
3. **Temperature drift** (§50) and **analogue noise floor** (§51). Neither is implemented; the signal path is fully deterministic and noiseless.
4. **A measured/derived BA662-style transconductance curve.** The VCA is `signal × envelope`, boosted by the accent control-current term, then passed through an asymmetric `tanh`. This gets the qualitative shape right (soft ceiling, not a hard linear multiply) but doesn't model an actual current-controlled-transconductance curve derived from the V662A reissue datasheet (§10 of the compendium).
5. **The dedicated Env Mod bias transistor (Q9) shifting the filter's DC operating point**, distinct from simply adding a CV term. The current implementation approximates this as an additive `cv_offset`/`cv_envmod` term in the same octave-CV sum as everything else, which gets the *qualitative* "small knob movement, big audible sweep" behavior in the right ballpark but doesn't model a literal bias-point shift interacting with the ladder's own nonlinear transfer curve.
6. **Resonance-dependent Accent Sweep time constant.** The real circuit's effective RC time constant for the accent-sweep capacitor varies continuously with the Resonance knob (via the 100 kΩ second-gang resistance) — the code uses one fixed 47 ms time constant for the capacitor regardless of Resonance, and instead expresses the Resonance/Accent-Sweep interaction entirely through a separate output-mixing weight (`resNorm * 0.7f` in `SynthEngine.cpp`). This is a reasonable approximation of the *result* but not the *mechanism*.
7. **Diode/transistor Vf asymmetry inside the filter.** The factory parts list includes at least one **germanium** diode (1S-188FM, Vf ≈ 0.3 V) alongside several **silicon** diode/transistor types (Vf ≈ 0.6 V), implying non-uniform forward-voltage behavior somewhere in the signal path. The filter's nonlinearity uses one uniform `Vt`/`tanh` shape for all four ladder stages, the new resonance-loop coupling-pole network, and the feedback path; no per-stage or per-junction Vf asymmetry is modeled. This would be cheap to add (a per-stage Vt multiplier) but there's no source for which stage(s) would use the germanium diode's lower Vf, so it's speculative rather than a known fix — flagged here as a "substantial guess" the user should treat with more skepticism than the coupling-pole frequency fix above.
8. **Most of the documented ≈6 further VCF coupling poles.** Only 1 sits inside the resonance feedback loop (150 Hz, see the Filter table above) plus 1 input-side and 1 output-side pole = 3 of ≈6. RobinSchmidt/Open303 gives a concrete template for the missing ones (a ~7.5 Hz notch, a ~14 Hz allpass, both outside the feedback loop) that hasn't been implemented yet — see "Biggest opportunities" above.
9. **Output/mixer coupling stage and stated output impedances** (10 kΩ line out, 8–30 Ω headphone out). Not relevant to a plugin's internal signal path, but worth noting as explicitly out of scope rather than accidentally omitted.
10. **Factory calibration-mode targets** (TM1–TM6 trim checkpoints, §11 of the compendium). There's no software "calibration mode" that could be validated against these checkpoints. (As of 2026-09, the plugin does expose a first set of hidden `vco_scale`/`filter_bias`-style calibration parameters — see the next section — but they're free-standing tuning knobs, not tied to the TM1–TM6 checkpoints specifically.)

## Performance note (2026-09 filter update)

All of the changes above are one-pole (or cascaded one-pole) linear filter stages evaluated once per oversampled tick inside `processFaithfulSample` — each costs roughly 2–4 FLOPs. At 8× oversampling and a typical host block/sample rate, this is immeasurably small next to the existing Newton-Raphson solve (3 iterations × a 4×4 tridiagonal solve, per oversampled tick) that already dominates the filter's cost. Since this is a monophonic synth (one filter instance active at a time, not one per voice in a polyphonic pool), there is substantial CPU headroom — none of the changes made here, nor the "cheap to extend further" follow-ups noted above (more coupling-pole stages, an Open303-style notch/allpass), are meaningful performance risks. The one change flagged above as **not** cheap is folding the coupling-pole network into the *implicit* Newton-Raphson solve itself (rather than treating it explicitly/pre-linearized, as done here) — that would enlarge the tridiagonal Jacobian to a pentadiagonal (or larger) system each Newton iteration, a real architectural change. It was deliberately not done: the coupling-pole's time constant (~1.1 ms at 150 Hz) is still large relative to one oversampled step (~2.8 μs at 8×/44.1kHz), so treating it explicitly costs negligible accuracy — there's no numerical-quality reason to pay for the implicit version.

## 2026-09-19: confirmed self-oscillation bug (fixed) -- resolved the render artifact too

A user-supplied render ("Faithful" mode, roughly Cutoff 0.28/Resonance 0.22/EnvMod 0.30/Decay 0.55/Accent 0.50, saw wave, a single held C2 note) showed a strong, spectrally **fixed** peak around 2650 Hz dominating the entire recording, regardless of the note played or its envelope — the classic signature of filter self-oscillation, not of a filtered sawtooth (whose loudest partial should be its own fundamental, or a resonance peak that tracks the cutoff/envelope). A same-settings Open303 render showed the expected, fundamental-dominated spectrum instead. Diagnosed with the new `tools/analyze_audio.py` (see below).

**Confirmed and fixed**: `src/filter_stability_test_main.cpp` (new; run it, see below) proved both `Filter::processAccurateSample` and `Filter::processFaithfulSample` **did self-oscillate** (failed to decay to silence after input stops) within the front panel's reachable 0.0–1.0 Resonance range — Accurate mode from Resonance≈0.6–0.8 depending on cutoff, Faithful mode from ≈0.7–1.0. This contradicted the "stock filter does not self-oscillate" research finding (`TB303_RESEARCH_COMPENDIUM.md` §6, Wikipedia's spec sheet). A subsequent detailed audit (`TB303_FILTER_AUDIT_2026-09-19.md`, supplied by the user from a parallel investigation) traced this to the exact root cause — feedback gain roughly 2x the ladder's analytically-derivable critical (self-oscillation) gain of 17 — which this session independently re-derived from scratch (the characteristic polynomial `s^4+8s^3+20s^2+16s+(2+2k)=0` and `k_critical=17` both confirmed by hand and numerically) and then implemented: see the Filter tables above for the specific fixes (feedback gain cap, resonance-knob skew, output-level compensation, capacitor-scale neutralization, cutoff-to-resonance-peak calibration). Post-fix, `syrebas_filter_stability_test` finds **zero self-oscillation across the entire 0.0–1.0 Resonance range at every tested cutoff**, in both modes.

**Resolved**: this fix eliminated the ~2650 Hz render artifact too, confirmed by the user re-testing. The reproduction attempts noted above (which couldn't trigger it from the read-off-the-screenshot knob settings) simply hadn't hit the right combination of cutoff/resonance to cross the pre-fix instability boundary in this project's own test harness — the fix itself, being a genuine correction of a real bug rather than a guess targeted at one file, covered the actual cause regardless.

**Follow-up (same day): knob-range mismatch.** After the self-oscillation fix, the user compared this project against an Open303-based reference (JC-303) at matching settings and found the two sounded close, but only when *this* project's Resonance and Env Mod knobs were maxed out where the reference needed only about half its travel for a similar effect — Cutoff matched fine. Diagnosed and fixed:
- **Resonance**: the self-oscillation fix's flat 15.3 ceiling, while safe everywhere, was far too conservative — it left almost all of the coupling-pole HPF's real stability headroom unused, so the resonant peak only reached ~2.5 dB above passband even at Resonance=1.0 (cutoff=2500 Hz). Empirically re-measured the *actual* per-cutoff stability boundary (bisection search against the real `Filter` class) and added `kCutoffHeadroomNumerator_` to reclaim it safely — see the Faithful-mode filter table above. Re-verified with `syrebas_filter_stability_test` (now sweeping 100 Hz–15 kHz): still zero self-oscillation everywhere, with the resonant peak now roughly doubled (~5 dB) at the same test point.
- **Env Mod**: was mapped through a squared ("quadratic audio taper") curve with no real justification. Open303's own measured Env Mod calibration (`calculateEnvModScalerAndOffset()`, its own comment: "arise from the measurements") is linear, not quadratic. Switched to linear — see the SynthEngine table above.

Both fixes were validated for stability (`syrebas_filter_stability_test`, `stress_test.cpp`-style parameter-extreme sweeps) before being reported back; neither has yet been re-validated by ear against the Open303 comparison at the *new* knob ranges, since only the user can do that.

New tooling committed alongside this investigation:
- `tools/analyze_audio.py` — reusable WAV spectral diagnostic (band-energy breakdown, fundamental-vs-overall-peak comparison, an explicit self-oscillation warning when the strongest peak doesn't track the fundamental across time). Works on any 16/24-bit PCM WAV, no dependencies. Intended for future render-vs-reference comparisons, including against real hardware recordings once isolated single-note/known-knob-position samples are available.
- `src/filter_stability_test_main.cpp` (build target `syrebas_filter_stability_test`) — sweeps cutoff × resonance for both filter modes and reports the self-oscillation boundary. Run this after any change to `Filter.cpp`'s feedback gain, coupling-pole, or capacitor-scale constants as a permanent regression check.

## 2026-09-19: Accent/Decay review — a systemic t60-vs-tau bug, plus two structural accent gaps

User request: "Can you have a review of accent and decay. I am not sure that these are correct either." Cross-checked `Envelope.hpp`/`.cpp` and the accent-related summing in `SynthEngine.cpp` against RobinSchmidt/Open303's actual envelope/accent source (`rosic_DecayEnvelope.cpp/.h`, `rosic_AnalogEnvelope.cpp/.h`, `rosic_Open303.cpp/.h`) — not read in earlier sessions, unlike the filter/oscillator code that was already cross-checked during the self-oscillation investigation.

**Finding 1 (high confidence, fixed): every "decay" time constant in `Envelope.cpp` was mistakenly treated as a t60 (time to -60 dB) and divided by 6.907755 to derive tau, making every decay ~6.9x faster than intended across its whole range.** Open303's two envelope classes (`DecayEnvelope`, used for the MEG/filter envelope; `AnalogEnvelope`, used for the VCA envelope) are both, without exception, driven **directly** by tau — `DecayEnvelope::calculateCoefficient()`: `c = exp(-1/(0.001*tau*fs))`, no t60 conversion anywhere; same RC convention in `AnalogEnvelope::setDecay()`/`setRelease()`. This is corroborated three independent ways:
- Open303's `setDecay()` doc comment states the real, un-modified 303 hardware's Decay-knob range as **"200...2000 ms"** — essentially identical to this project's own already-researched range (0.2–2.5 s, `TB303_EMULATION_REFERENCE.md` §19.2) — fed into `DecayEnvelope` as tau with zero conversion.
- Open303's `accentDecay = 200.0` (ms) literal constant, also fed as tau with zero conversion, is numerically identical to this project's own `kAccentDecayTimeSec = 0.20f` (200 ms) — a constant this project derived independently, from a different source, for the same accent-decay-override behavior.
- Open303's `setAmpDecay()` doc comment states the real, un-modified 303's VCA decay was **"fixed to approximately 3-4 seconds"** — closely matching this project's own already-existing 3.5 s default for `vegDecaySec_` (previously only sourced to Whittle's vaguer "rather long").

In all three cases, the *numeric ranges/defaults already in this project's code and docs were right* — the bug was purely the extra `/6.907755f` unit conversion applied on top, which silently made every MEG/VEG decay ~6.9x too fast. Fixed by removing the conversion from `vcfDecayCoeff_` (both the normal and the faithful-mode accent-override branches) and `vcaGateHighDecayCoeff_` in `Envelope.cpp::updateCoefficients()`; `vcfDecayTimeSec_`/`kAccentDecayTimeSec`/`vegDecaySec_` are now used directly as tau, matching Open303 exactly. Verified numerically with a standalone harness (measuring actual t60 against each target tau): all four cases (Decay knob min, Decay knob max, accent-decay override, VEG decay) land on their intended tau within measurement slop. This is very likely the single largest contributor to the user's "decay doesn't sound right" impression — envelope sweeps and VCA decay were audibly far more clipped/percussive than intended across the entire Decay knob range.

**Finding 2 (well-evidenced, fixed): non-accented and accented note-offs previously used one shared VCA "quick drain" time; Open303 gives them clearly different values.** Same t60-vs-tau bug applied here too (`vcaQuickDrainCoeff_`), plus a missing accent/non-accent split: Open303's `triggerNote()` sets `ampEn.setRelease(normalAmpRelease=1.0ms)` for a non-accented note but `ampEnv.setRelease(accentAmpRelease=50.0ms)` for an accented one — a deliberate asymmetry (accented notes "hang" audibly longer after note-off; non-accented notes cut off almost instantly), not just a magnitude tweak. Fixed: split `Envelope::vcaGateOffSec_` (now 1 ms default, was 16 ms, matching `normalAmpRelease` once corrected) from a new `vcaGateOffAccentSec_` (50 ms default, matching `accentAmpRelease`), selected by `isAccent_` at the moment the gate goes low. Both are now separate CLAP parameters (`VCA Gate-Off Tail` / `VCA Gate-Off Tail (Accent)`, `Experimental/Envelope`). This is a significant, audible change to the default sound of every non-accented note-off (much snappier/clickier than before) — flagged clearly since it hasn't been validated by ear yet.
>
> **Superseded 2026-09-20 (see the dated section near the end of this document): direct hardware A/B found no such asymmetry.** With both `VCA Gate-Off Tail` and `VCA Gate-Off Tail (Accent)` swept live against a reference hardware track, the project owner found both need the *same* ~3 ms value to match the hardware's note-off — an accented note's amplitude reaches zero exactly as fast as a non-accented one on this unit, contradicting Open303's `accentAmpRelease=50.0` asymmetry entirely. This finding's "well-evidenced" tag rested on Open303's source code, not on any independent hardware measurement or primary 303 documentation -- Open303 is itself an emulation (RobinSchmidt's, inspired by but not a transistor-level clone of the real circuit), so `accentAmpRelease` was always that project's own calibration choice, not a verified hardware constant, and evidently doesn't hold for this hardware unit. Defaults for both parameters are now 3 ms; see the 2026-09-20 section below for the measurement and what's still open (whether *other* accent-only behavior, like the separate MEG decay override below, holds up any better).

**Finding 3 (well-evidenced, fixed): non-accented notes had zero envelope-to-VCA coupling; on real hardware/Open303 they should have some.** Open303's VCA mixing (`rosic_Open303.h::getSample()`): `ampEnvOut += 0.45*mainEnvOut + accentGain*4.0*mainEnvOut`, applied whenever a note is on — i.e. the filter/MEG envelope leaks into the VCA on **every** note (giving all notes a bit of percussive "snap" tied to the filter sweep), with accent adding a much larger *additional* contribution on top (not replacing the baseline). This project's `SynthEngine.cpp` previously only added a VCA contribution `if (noteAccent)` — non-accented notes got none of this coupling at all. Fixed by adding an unconditional `vcaGain += 0.45f * vcfEnvVal;` baseline term (matching Open303's `0.45` coefficient exactly), leaving the existing accented-note term unchanged and additive on top, same as Open303's structure.

**Not changed (weaker evidence / already correct):**
- The Accent Sweep's resonance-gang interaction (`directAccentPortion`/`sweepCapPortion`, `resNorm * 0.7f` blend in `SynthEngine.cpp`) has no Open303 equivalent to cross-check against at all — Open303 doesn't model the dual-gang Resonance pot's coupling into the accent sweep, so its absence there isn't evidence either way. Left as-is (still 🔴 unsourced per the SynthEngine table above).
- Faithful mode's "accent forces Env Mod depth to 100%, unconditionally" behavior (`effectiveEnvMod` in `SynthEngine.cpp`) is structurally different from Open303, which never overrides its envelope-scaler on accent — it only adds a separate, smaller, purely-accent-driven term on top (the `tmp2`/`accentGain*4.0` path above), while the normal envelope contribution (`tmp1`/`envScaler`) stays exactly as the Env Mod knob dictates regardless of accent. This is a real structural discrepancy worth further scrutiny, but it's tied to a specifically-cited research section (§15/§24) rather than being a self-evident bug the way the t60/tau issue was, and changing it would alter Faithful mode's cutoff-sweep character on every accented note — left untouched this round, flagged here for a future, more targeted investigation (ideally informed by listening, or a firmer primary source than what's cited).
- Attack-phase coefficients (`vcfAttackCoeff_`, `vcaAttackCoeff_`, the 47 ms/1.55 ms accent-sweep RC times) were already using the direct-tau convention correctly — no `/6.907755f` was ever present on those, only on the decay-side coefficients.

Verification: full project build is clean; `syrebas_filter_stability_test` still reports zero self-oscillation across the full grid (unaffected by these envelope-only changes, run as a sanity check); a new standalone harness (`envelope_decay_check.cpp`, scratch-only, not committed) confirmed all four corrected tau values land on target. None of this has been validated by ear yet — in particular, the non-accented note-off change (16 ms → 1 ms) is a significant, easily-noticed change to every note's release transient and is worth listening to specifically.

## 2026-09-19 (later): topology pass -- pushing toward a pure calibration problem

**Goal, per the project owner:** stop treating remaining inaccuracy as "maybe it's a wrong constant, maybe it's a structural gap" and instead fix every structural/topological gap this session could identify from the sources already gathered here, so that what's left is *purely* a parameter-fitting problem an external tool (planned separately, out of scope for this session) can solve by optimizing against reference audio (e.g. a trusted commercial 303 emulation). This section documents what changed and why each change is a **topology** fix (a structural gap the docs above call out, not a new guess at a number), plus the new build-time calibration-parameter surface that exposes every touched constant for that future optimizer.

**1. Oscillator aliasing (bug, not a calibration gap).** `Oscillator::processNextSample` generated the square wave as a raw discontinuous step and the saw as an ideal ramp fed through only a gentle one-pole 14 kHz smoothing -- neither is band-limited. EMULATION_GUIDE.md §55 already flags this class of problem explicitly ("Simply running a nonlinear oscillator at 44.1 kHz with no oversampling will generate aliasing that was not present in the original analogue hardware"), and it's a *bug* in the sense that no constant retuning fixes a discontinuity's aliasing -- you need actual band-limited synthesis. Added a standard PolyBLEP (polynomial band-limited step) correction at the saw's wrap discontinuity and at both edges of the square, applied at host sample rate (no oversampling needed for this technique). No new parameters -- this is a correctness fix, not a knob.

**2. Missing VCF coupling poles outside the resonance loop.** The compendium's own §16 addendum already concluded the "six further poles" claim should be realized as an Open303-templated post-filter highpass (~24 Hz), notch (~7.5 Hz, bandwidth ~4.7) and allpass (~14 Hz), all **outside** the resonance feedback loop's gain path (unlike the in-loop coupling-pole HPF, which was already implemented) -- and flagged this as "the most promising next step" (Biggest Opportunities #3), not yet attempted. Implemented now: `Filter.hpp` gained `OnePoleAllpass` and a small RBJ-cookbook `NotchFilter`, applied once per host-rate sample (not oversampled -- their time constants are tens of ms, far too slow to need it) right after the oversampled ladder solve. New calibration parameters: `Filter Post HP Freq`, `Filter Notch Freq`, `Filter Notch Bandwidth`, `Filter Allpass Freq` (defaults from Open303's shipped values, per the same cross-referencing rationale used throughout this document).

**3. Accent forcing Env Mod to 100% (structural bug, previously flagged and left unfixed).** `SynthEngine.cpp` computed `effectiveEnvMod = noteAccent ? 1.0f : envModNorm`, i.e. an accented note discarded the Env Mod knob's setting entirely. This was already identified as a real structural discrepancy in the "2026-09-19: Accent/Decay review" section above ("Faithful mode's accent-forces-100%... structurally different from Open303, which never overrides its envelope-scaler on accent — it only adds a separate, smaller, purely-accent-driven term on top") but left untouched pending "a more targeted investigation." That investigation is this section: the override conflated two separate, independently-documented mechanisms (MEG decay override, which is real and already correctly implemented via `setFaithfulAccentDecay`, and the Accent Sweep circuit, which is a *parallel additive* current path per compendium §9, not a bypass of Env Mod). Fixed: `cv_envmod` now always scales by the Env Mod knob (`envModNorm`), on every note; the Accent Sweep's own contribution (`cv_accent`) is unchanged in kind, just no longer stacked on top of an inflated Env Mod term.

**4. Accent Sweep resonance-gang blend artificially capped at 70% of its travel.** `directAccentPortion`/`sweepCapPortion` were weighted by `resNorm * 0.7f`, so even at Resonance = 1.0 the accent signal was only 70% capacitor-smoothed, 30% direct -- compendium §9 describes the wiper reaching (near-)fully direct at one end and (near-)fully capacitor-smoothed at the other. Changed the blend to span the pot's full documented travel: `(1 - resNorm)` direct / `resNorm` capacitor-smoothed.

**5. Accent Sweep signal leaking into non-accented notes.** The non-accented branch still injected `accentNorm * sweepCapPortion * 0.75f` into the filter's cutoff current -- but compendium §9 opens with "all sourced from the MEG through a switch that is only closed during accented steps": the whole Accent Sweep path (not just the MEG-decay override) is switch-gated and should contribute nothing to the filter when the current step isn't accented. Fixed: `cv_accent` is now exactly `0` on non-accented notes. The capacitor's own charge/discharge state (`Envelope::accentCap_`) is untouched by this change and still persists and decays between notes exactly as before -- it's only the *readout* into the filter's summing node that's now gated, which is what actually produces the documented "rising peaks across consecutive accents" behavior (a non-accented note in between should not siphon off the stored charge into the filter).

**6. Unsupported resonance→cutoff CV "bleed" term removed.** `SynthEngine.cpp` reduced `effectiveCutoff` by up to 15% as Resonance increased, via a term (`resCutoffBleed`) explicitly logged in this document as "not addressed by any source consulted... a candidate for removal." Since the goal of this pass is a topology that's justified by the documented circuit interactions (the Accent-Sweep/Resonance-gang coupling already reproduces a real, sourced Resonance/Accent interaction; this bleed term was an unrelated, invented DSP-level shortcut with no claimed circuit mechanism at all), it's been removed rather than kept as a knob. `PARAM_RES_CUTOFF_BLEED` no longer exists; the freed CLAP parameter slot was reused for the new filter coupling-pole parameters (see below) -- low-risk since this ID was already restricted to the experimental/calibration surface, never on the front panel.

**7. VCA modeled as a single output-stage nonlinearity, not a saturating transconductance gain stage.** EMULATION_REFERENCE.md §23 is explicit that the BA662 "should not simply be represented by `output = input * envelope`... nonlinear current-to-gain behavior, saturation at high signal/control levels" -- but the code computed a linear `vcaGain` (up to ~2.25 at full accent) and only saturated the *combined* `filterOut * vcaGain` product in one shared asymmetric `tanh`, which conflates the amplifier's own control-current saturation with the following buffer stage's signal clipping. Split into two distinct nonlinearities: `vcaGain` is now `tanh(vcaControl * drive) / tanh(drive)` (a control-current-referenced saturating gain stage, unity at `vcaControl == 1`, drive-controlled compression as it approaches/exceeds that), followed by the existing asymmetric output-stage `tanh` on the resulting signal. New calibration parameter: `VCA Gain Saturation Drive` (plausible range 1-8, default 3.0). **Flag for the future optimizer/ear-check:** this compresses the accent-driven gain boost much more than before by default (a heavily accented note's control current now saturates toward roughly unity gain instead of reaching ~2.25x) -- exactly the kind of magnitude question this whole pass is trying to hand off to calibration fitting rather than resolve by further guessing here.

**Build-time calibration surface.** Added a `ACIDUS_CALIBRATION_BUILD` CMake option (see README.md). Off (default): `AcidusClap` registers only the 7 front-panel parameters, matching the real hardware's user-facing surface exactly -- a Release build's plugin looks and automates identically to before this pass, modulo the DSP topology fixes above. On: all 11 experimental/calibration constants (the pre-existing 7 plus this pass's 4 new ones) are registered as ordinary automatable CLAP parameters under `Experimental/...` module paths, for a future external optimizer to drive against reference audio, as originally sketched by EMULATION_GUIDE.md §53's "Hidden Calibration Parameters" list. The underlying `SynthParameters`/`Filter`/`Envelope` API is identical between the two builds; only which constants are exposed as `clap_param_info_t` entries differs (`AcidusClap.hpp`'s `PARAM_COUNT` is compile-time gated by the macro).

**What this pass deliberately did *not* do**, and why: per-instance component variation/thermal drift/noise floor (EMULATION_REFERENCE.md §49-51) was left out on purpose -- a non-deterministic model is harder, not easier, for a future deterministic-audio-matching optimizer to fit against a fixed reference render, so it belongs *after* calibration against a clean reference, not before. Per-stage diode Vf asymmetry inside the ladder (EMULATION_REFERENCE §7's "not emulated" list, item 7) was left out too: the compendium itself calls this "a substantial guess... more skepticism than the coupling-pole frequency fix," with no source for which stage would even use the lower-Vf germanium diode, so adding it now would just be inventing another unjustified structural degree of freedom rather than closing a documented gap.

**Verification:** both build configurations (`-DACIDUS_CALIBRATION_BUILD=OFF` default and `=ON`) compile cleanly. `acidus_filter_stability_test` still reports zero self-oscillation across the full 100 Hz-15 kHz / 0.0-1.0 Resonance grid (expected -- the new coupling poles sit outside the resonance feedback loop and can't affect its stability boundary). `acidus_dsp_test`'s render remains finite, non-clipping, and fundamental-dominated per `tools/analyze_audio.py` (no self-oscillation signature introduced). None of this has been validated by ear against a reference recording yet -- per this document's own recurring methodological lesson, treat every default value touched here (especially the VCA saturation drive and the new filter coupling-pole corners) as a starting point for the planned calibration-fitting tool, not a conclusion.

## 2026-09-20 (later): Reference-track A/B -- Finding 3's unconditional MEG->VCA leak reverted; ladder pole spread exposed

**Goal, per the project owner:** direct A/B against a hardware TB-303 test track (waveform + spectrum comparison at matched knob settings, including a "Decay max" single-note test and a short sequenced bassline mixing accented and non-accented steps) surfaced two audible mismatches, addressed in order as they were found.

**1. `vcaControl`'s unconditional `0.45f * vcfEnvVal` baseline term (bug against measured hardware, contradicts "2026-09-19: Accent/Decay review" Finding 3 above) -- removed.** With the hardware unit's Decay knob at max and a single non-accented note held then released, the hardware's VCA cut off promptly after note-off; Acidus's did not -- the note kept sounding for as long as the MEG's own Decay-controlled decay was still running (up to ~2.5 s), because `SynthEngine.cpp`'s `vcaControl` included an *unconditional* `0.45f * vcfEnvVal` term applied on every note regardless of gate state, and `vcfEnvVal` (the MEG) is deliberately free-running and gate-independent (§20, "MEG Gate-Off Behavior" -- correct on its own terms). That unconditional term was added deliberately in Finding 3 above, sourced from Open303's `rosic_Open303.h::getSample()`: `ampEnvOut += 0.45*mainEnvOut + accentGain*4.0*mainEnvOut`, applied on every note. First pass gated it to accent-only (matching the *structure* of Open303's formula, where the baseline term is still unconditional but at least confining the regression to accented notes) -- but a second reference-track pass, this time sequencing accented and non-accented steps together, showed accented notes still not releasing at all, for the same underlying reason: the accent-gated `0.45f * vcfEnvVal` still isn't scaled by the Accent knob (`accentNorm`) and still doesn't respect gate-off, since it's reading the free-running MEG directly.

Removed the term entirely rather than re-gating it again. `EMULATION_REFERENCE.md` §26 ("Accent VCA Control") only ever documented *one* MEG->VCA path -- "through additional analogue filtering, including a network associated with approximately 47 kΩ + 0.033 µF, which softens the accent contribution" -- which is exactly `accentVcaVal` (an RC lowpass of the MEG, `Envelope.cpp`'s `accentVcaCoeff_`, ~1.55 ms tau, target forced to 0 the instant gate goes low, already scaled by `accentNorm` in `SynthEngine.cpp`). §26 never describes a second, unconditional, un-softened MEG leak on top of that -- Finding 3's addition came from Open303's implementation, not from this project's own hardware-sourced research, and on this specific point Open303's structure does not reproduce what the reference hardware track actually does. `vcaControl` is now `vcaEnvVal` on every note, plus `accentVcaVal * accentNorm * 0.8f` on accented notes only -- i.e. exactly §26's single documented path, nothing more. This is a deliberate, evidence-based departure from Open303 on this one term, not an oversight; Finding 3's other content (the `AnalogEnvelope`/`DecayEnvelope` tau convention, the accent gate-off asymmetry) is unaffected and still stands.

Verified with a standalone harness driving `Envelope` directly (decay knob at max, accent knob at both extremes): non-accented note-off now reaches ~0 VCA envelope within ~10-15 ms (`vcaGateOffSec_` tau); accented note-off now decays cleanly on `vcaGateOffAccentSec_`'s own 50 ms tau (`accentVcaVal` itself already collapses within ~10-15 ms via its 1.55 ms RC, independent of the Accent knob's position) with no residual sustain plateau at any Accent knob setting, including Accent knob at minimum.

**2. Ladder pole-frequency spread (`Filter.hpp`'s `capScale1_..4_`) exposed as calibration parameters.** Separately, the reference track's spectrum showed a clearly audible 3rd-harmonic peak even at a fairly low cutoff with Resonance maxed, where Acidus's spectrum stayed fundamental-dominated until cutoff was pushed much higher. `capScale1_..4_` were hardcoded to `1.0` (coincident ladder poles -- the idealized equal-component approximation EMULATION_REFERENCE.md §6.1 describes as a simplification, not the real filter, which EMULATION_GUIDE.md and RESEARCH_COMPENDIUM.md §6 both describe as having unevenly spread pole frequencies). No sourced target values exist for the spread (RESEARCH_COMPENDIUM.md §6's illustrative normalized poles -0.13/-1.04/-2.33/-3.24 are explicitly flagged unsourced), so rather than guess a spread, added `Filter::setCapScale1..4()` and four new `Experimental/Filter` calibration parameters (`Filter Ladder Pole Scale 1..4`, range 0.2-4.0, default 1.0 -- Release-build behavior unchanged) so the spread can be fit against the reference track directly, the same calibration-surface pattern used throughout the 2026-09-19 topology pass above.

**Verification:** all touched files compile cleanly standalone (`g++ -std=c++17`) and `AcidusClap.cpp` compiles cleanly in both `ACIDUS_CALIBRATION_BUILD` configurations. Not yet validated against the reference track by ear/eye for the accent fix, and the pole-spread parameters are un-fit defaults (1.0, i.e. no change from prior behavior) awaiting the project owner's own sweep against the hardware spectrum.

## 2026-09-20 (yet later): accent VCA release asymmetry retracted; resonance-peak amplitude dependence found; ladder drive exposed

**1. `VCA Gate-Off Tail (Accent)` asymmetry retracted -- accented and non-accented note-offs measure identically (~3 ms) on the reference hardware.** Following up on the widened range above, the project owner swept both `VCA Gate-Off Tail` and `VCA Gate-Off Tail (Accent)` live against the reference track and found **3 ms for both** matches hardware -- there is no accent/non-accent release-time asymmetry on this unit at all. This directly contradicts "Finding 2" in the 2026-09-19 section above, which added the 1 ms/50 ms split specifically *because* Open303's source (`normalAmpRelease=1.0` vs `accentAmpRelease=50.0`) states it as "a deliberate asymmetry." That citation was accurate -- Open303's code really does do this -- but Open303 is itself a soft-synth emulation (RobinSchmidt's), not a hardware measurement or a transistor-level clone; `accentAmpRelease=50.0` was always that project's own calibration choice, not a verified constant from real hardware, and it evidently doesn't hold for this specific unit. Lesson for this document going forward: a citation to Open303's source code is evidence about what Open303 does, not automatically evidence about what the real 303 does -- the two can and do diverge, and only a direct hardware A/B (as done here) settles it. Both `Envelope::vcaGateOffSec_` and `vcaGateOffAccentSec_` defaults are now 3 ms (`SynthEngine.hpp`, `AcidusClap.cpp`'s calibration-build init and `paramsInfo` defaults). The two separate parameters and the `isAccent_`-selected coefficient branch in `Envelope::processNextSample()` are left in place rather than collapsed into one constant -- they cost nothing to keep and preserve the ability to reintroduce a measured asymmetry later if a different unit or a firmer source ever calls for one.

Explicitly **not** changed by this finding: the separate MEG decay override (`kAccentDecayTimeSec = 0.20f`, fixed ~200 ms for accented notes' *filter* envelope, independent of the Decay knob) -- that's a different circuit path (cutoff sweep / timbre, not VCA loudness) and this measurement was specifically about note-off *loudness* reaching zero, not about the filter's decay character. Whether that 200 ms figure holds up against hardware is still an open question, per the "Also unresolved" note directly above.

**2. Resonant peak height found to be strongly signal-amplitude-dependent -- not primarily a feedback-gain-margin issue.** The project owner's hardware/Acidus spectrum comparison (cutoff near max of its front-panel-reachable range, i.e. ≈2500 Hz per `cv_base`'s formula with Env Mod at 0, Resonance = 1.0) showed the hardware's resonant peak roughly 15 dB taller than Acidus's at the same settings. Two things were checked:

- **Feedback-gain margin (`kResonanceGainMargin_ = 0.90`, hardcoded) has only a small effect.** A standalone sweep of `Filter::feedbackGainCeiling_` (already an exposed calibration parameter, range 12-17, default 15.3) against the *actual* nonlinear `Filter` class (not the idealized linear model) at cutoff=2500 Hz, Resonance=1.0 found: peak height stays roughly flat (~10-13 dB with a small-signal probe) from the current default up through the analytically-derived critical gain of 17.0, then the model goes unstable (confirmed self-oscillating, `rmsLate` fails to decay) between ceiling=17.0 and 17.3. So the exposed `Filter Feedback Gain` parameter's headroom is nearly exhausted already -- pushing it to its current max (17.0) is safe and free to try but recovers only ~1-2 dB, not 15.
- **Signal amplitude drives a much larger effect.** The same sweep repeated at increasing probe amplitude (0.05 quiet-probe up to 1.0, matching `Oscillator::processNextSample()`'s actual full-scale output) found peak height collapses from ~12.9 dB at 0.05 amplitude down to **essentially 0 dB (no audible resonance) at 1.0 amplitude**, independent of the feedback-gain ceiling. Root cause: `Filter.cpp` scales the input into each ladder stage's `tanh()` nonlinearity by a literal `0.05` against a thermal-voltage-like constant `Vt = 0.052` -- since these are nearly equal, a full-scale musical signal (~1.0) lands almost exactly at the nonlinearity's saturation onset, so the *raw* signal is already heavily compressed by the per-stage diode-conduction model before resonance is even considered, and that compression crushes the resonant peak's visibility along with everything else. This is a substantially bigger and previously-unflagged effect compared to the margin question above, and better explains a 15 dB gap.

  Exposed the `0.05` literal as `Filter::ladderInputScale_` (new setter `setLadderInputScale()`, used for both the input scale-down and the reciprocal output scale-up, so passband gain is unaffected as this is swept) and registered it as a new `Experimental/Filter` CLAP parameter, `Filter Ladder Input Drive`, range 0.02-0.20, default 0.05 (unchanged Release-build behavior). Lowering it gives the resonance peak more headroom before the per-stage saturation kicks in; this trades off against the "warmth"/compression character that same saturation is presumably also responsible for, so finding the right value needs the reference track, not a guess -- not attempted here.

**Verification:** all touched files (`Filter.hpp`/`.cpp`, `SynthEngine.hpp`/`.cpp`, `AcidusClap.hpp`/`.cpp`) compile cleanly standalone and `AcidusClap.cpp` compiles cleanly in both `ACIDUS_CALIBRATION_BUILD` configurations. A standalone harness confirmed the two VCA gate-off taus now produce matched, symmetric ~10-12 ms release curves for accented and non-accented notes at their new 3 ms defaults. The resonance/amplitude finding is diagnostic only -- `Filter Ladder Input Drive` is an unfit default (0.05, no change from prior behavior) awaiting the project owner's own sweep against the hardware spectrum, same as the ladder pole-spread parameters above.

## 2026-09-20 (final entry this session): pole-scale/feedback-gain coupling confirmed; offline resonance-fit optimizer added

**1. Changing `capScale1..4` away from uniform silently moves the instability boundary -- confirmed by direct measurement, not just theory.** The project owner's own live experimentation (capScale = 0.71/1.08/1.45/1.03, `Filter Feedback Gain` left at its uniform-pole-calibrated default of 15.3) produced audibly "ugly" extra harmonics at ~2x/3x/4x the resonant peak frequency. A standalone bisection against that exact pole configuration found the true instability boundary had dropped from 17.0 (the uniform-pole value everything else in this document is calibrated against) to **~15.7-15.8** -- i.e. the default `Filter Feedback Gain` was running at ~97% of the *actual* critical gain for that configuration, not the intended ~90% margin, which is consistent with the harmonics being a near-self-oscillation artifact rather than clean coloration. A follow-up attempt (capScale reset to uniform 1.0, `Filter Ladder Input Drive` dropped to 0.02, `Filter Feedback Gain` dropped to 14) produced a much *shorter* resonant peak (-18 dB vs. the hardware's -2.5 dB) than earlier attempts -- expected, since that combination simultaneously reverted the pole spread (which the earlier config was using for peak sharpness) and dropped the margin fraction (14/17 = 82%) below what previous configurations were using, i.e. multiple free parameters were changed at once, making it impossible to attribute the result to any one of them. **Practical conclusion for anyone calibrating this filter by hand:** `capScale1..4` and `Filter Feedback Gain` are coupled -- after any pole-scale change, re-find the safe `Filter Feedback Gain` ceiling for that specific configuration (e.g. via `acidus_filter_stability_test`-style bisection) rather than assuming the previous ceiling still applies.

**2. Added `acidus_filter_resonance_fit` (`src/filter_resonance_fit_main.cpp`), a standalone offline optimizer.** Given how tightly coupled `capScale1..4`, `Filter Feedback Gain`, and `Filter Ladder Input Drive` turned out to be (6 free parameters total, at least 3 of them interacting in non-obvious ways), hand-tuning one knob at a time was no longer a productive strategy -- the project owner asked for a tool that searches all of them jointly against a target spectrum instead. The tool:
- Runs a from-scratch Nelder-Mead simplex search directly against the real, nonlinear `Filter` class (not a linearized/idealized model), so the fit accounts for the same saturation and instability effects a human ear runs into.
- Takes target (frequency, relative-dB) landmark points from a text file (`tools/resonance_targets_example.txt` documents the format and how to derive real points from a hardware recording), all expressed relative to the first listed point (conventionally the fundamental).
- Rejects any candidate parameter set that self-oscillates (via the same burst-decay stability test used elsewhere in this project) with a heavy penalty, so the search can't "cheat" its way to a target by drifting into instability.
- Reports the best-found `Filter Ladder Pole Scale 1..4` / `Filter Feedback Gain` / `Filter Ladder Input Drive` values, plus a final high-precision (long-settle-time) re-check against each target point.

**Important limitation, demonstrated by the tool's own first run:** a smoke-test run against a 3-point placeholder target (fundamental=0dB reference, ~1.7kHz plateau=-16dB, ~3.1kHz peak=-2.5dB -- landmark values reported by ear/eye earlier this session, not a precise measurement) converged toward physically implausible pole-scale values (several approaching or exceeding the tool's own soft bound of ~4-5x) while still leaving one target point ~9-10 dB off. Three points cannot uniquely constrain 6 free parameters -- many different, including implausible, parameter combinations can satisfy 2-3 landmark points while getting everything in between wrong. **The tool needs a much richer target set to produce a trustworthy fit** -- ideally a dozen-plus (frequency, dB) points read directly off an FFT of an isolated single-note hardware recording at fixed knob settings (Env Mod near 0, so the cutoff isn't sweeping during the measurement), not points estimated from a live spectrum-analyzer screenshot. Do not treat this tool's output as a final answer until it's been run against a real, dense target derived from a hardware recording -- treat early runs as validation that the search mechanics work, not as calibration results.

**Verification:** `acidus_filter_resonance_fit` builds cleanly through the real CMake build (`cmake --build . --target acidus_filter_resonance_fit`), compiles with `-Wall` clean, and a 150-iteration run against the placeholder target completes in a few minutes on this environment's hardware.

`accentVcaDepth`, `accentDecaySec` -- are still unconstrained because every
reference has Env Mod at minimum and Decay at maximum, so applying their
fitted values would extrapolate onto completely untested knob positions. A
sanity check applying *all* 34 anyway (not committed) confirmed this
matters: `envModDepthOct` alone would have dropped from 3.5 to 0.78 octaves,
gutting the Env Mod knob's effect for a value no sample can see.

Remaining known gaps, unchanged from before: the resonant peak at max
Resonance is still 6-10 dB below hardware (feedback gain actually *dropped*
in this fit, 15.3 -> 9.8, trading peak height for a smoother rolloff
elsewhere -- the peak-height/rolloff-smoothness tradeoff looks like a real
model limitation, not just a search artifact, since a lighter smoothness
prior barely moved the fit). Spurious high-frequency content above 4 kHz
persists at high Resonance (worst case +21 dB on the accented low note,
`303_saw-D3t-39c1r1e0d1a1`), and that sample's free-knob refit prefers
Env Mod > 0 / Accent off over its own label, so its accent-step assumption
may be wrong. `303_saw-D2t-39c1r1e0d1a1` remains the worst-fitting sample
overall (2x the median error) -- its resonant sweep and ~7 dB level decay
over the note are still not reproduced by the model.

## 2026-09-26 (later still): removed the resonance-output-gain crutch; peak now comes from real feedback gain

User feedback after the previous re-fit: the resonant peak had all but
disappeared -- "moving away from the classic 303 sound." Root cause found
in this project's own docs: `Filter.cpp` had a flat, Resonance-scaled output
gain applied after the ladder (`filterMaxResonanceOutputGain`), which
`TB303_EMULATION_REFERENCE.md` Sec60 explicitly says not to add ("do not
add a separate 'bass compensation' stage"; passband gain is supposed to
*fall* with Resonance, not get uniformly boosted). That stage gave every
prior fit a cheap way to raise the level of Resonance=max samples without
sharpening the feedback loop's actual peak, and the previous re-fit used
it: `filterFeedbackGain` dropped from 15.3 to 9.8, well short of the
ladder's self-oscillation ceiling (`kLadderCriticalGain_` = 17).

Removed the output-gain stage entirely (Filter.hpp/.cpp) and widened
`filterFeedbackGain`'s search range (6-22 -> 6-30) so the bound itself
can't cap how close it gets to critical; an actually-unstable render is
already rejected during fitting (amplitude/`isfinite` check). Also added a
dedicated resonant-peak feature to `calibrate_reference.py`
(`peak_window`/`local_bump` in the script): for every Resonance=max sample,
detect the true resonant bump as a local deviation over a smoothed
baseline (not just the loudest harmonic overall -- at high cutoff the
bump is often quieter than the fundamental, which a plain argmax mistakes
for "no peak, perfect match"), then compare a window of harmonics around
it against the model, weighted 2x a plain harmonic term by default so
broadband fitting can no longer trade it away.

Result: `filterFeedbackGain` fit to 17.04 -- right at the documented
self-oscillation ceiling, consistent with the hardware's own documented
"approaches but does not cleanly self-oscillate" behavior (Sec12) -- and
the filter stability sweep still shows no self-oscillation at any tested
cutoff/resonance after applying it. Resonant-peak shape error dropped
9.31 dB -> 6.54 dB and a real, correctly-located peak now appears on most
Resonance=max samples (e.g. `303_saw-A2t-39c1r1e0d1a0`: hardware peak at
3117 Hz / -3.6 dB below the fundamental, fitted model now 3009 Hz / -8.3
dB -- present and close, versus the previous fit's flat non-answer).

Remaining gaps: peak height is still typically 3-6 dB shy of hardware, and
two samples don't land the frequency right -- `303_saw-D2t-39c1r1e0d1a0`
(fitted peak stuck at ~3 kHz vs hardware's 1934 Hz) and
`303_saw-D3t-39c1r1e0d1a1` (fitted ~6167 Hz vs hardware's 3155 Hz, an
octave off). The latter is also the run's most-suspect sample by error
and by free-knob refit (fits 74% better with much less Accent/Decay than
its label claims), so treat its knob labels as unconfirmed rather than
treating this as a pure model gap. `filterCapScale1..4` are now well-
constrained by the data (sensitivity 0.42-0.67, versus 0.02-0.06 before
the peak feature existed) at 0.28/0.71/0.34/0.53 -- still an uneven spread,
but no longer close to arbitrary.
